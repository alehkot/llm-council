A neighborhood bakery must decide whether to add a second oven before the holiday season.

The current oven can produce 400 items per day. Expected demand is 380 items on 20 normal
weekdays and 560 items on 8 peak days per month. Each additional item sold contributes $3
after ingredients and packaging, before equipment and additional labor costs. Unsold goods
have no salvage value. The existing oven and shop lease are sunk costs for this decision.

Options:
- Keep the existing oven and decline orders above capacity.
- Rent an additional oven for a two-month trial at $1,200 per month plus $600 one-time
  delivery and setup. It adds 200 items per day and can be returned without penalty.
- Buy the same additional capacity for $18,000 plus $1,000 installation. Assume a three-year
  useful life, zero salvage value, no financing, and $150 monthly maintenance.

Either added oven requires $90 in additional labor on each day it is used. There is no
additional labor cost on days it is idle. Assume it is used only when demand exceeds 400.
The demand forecast might be 25% too high or 25% too low; apply those multipliers to both
normal-day and peak-day demand. Assume 28 operating days per month and ignore taxes.

Recommend an option. Show monthly incremental contribution, rental break-even volume,
purchase payback, and sensitivity to the low/base/high demand scenarios. Distinguish
cash flow from accounting cost. Identify assumptions, practical risks, and a reversible
experiment with explicit continue/stop criteria. Avoid assuming the holiday demand
continues all year. End with a concrete two-month action plan and the key missing facts
that would change your decision.
